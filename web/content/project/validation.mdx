# Validation

[The milestones](/project/milestones/) sign M6 off when "resistance calls match
published results ... with discrepancies explained". This is what was compared, what
agreed, and what did not.

Everything here is reproduced by `tests/test_validation.py`, which runs the
real pipeline over real reads on every CI run of the `demo` job.

## Two things are validated, separately

**The scoring engine** is checked against Stanford's own answers.
`crates/ligase-core/tests/fixtures/hivdb/` holds the Stanford HIVdb reports
published as supplementary data by the HIVGenoPipe study — thirty clinical
samples at four ambiguity thresholds, 3,348 expectations — produced by
Sierra 3.5.3 against HIVDB 9.8.

**All 3,348 are reproduced exactly**: every drug, every score, every level.

That oracle matters because the obvious alternative is circular. Checking
the scorer against expectations derived from the same XML it reads would
test the parser against itself. Sierra is a different implementation, in a
different language, by the algorithm's own authors.

**The pipeline end to end** is checked by running it on reads whose
published answers are known, which is the rest of this page.

## The samples

PRJNA1218827 is the HIVGenoPipe study's MiSeq data. Four runs are fetched,
and the SRA metadata maps each to the sample identifier the paper uses —
so nothing rests on reading a figure.

| Run | Sample | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| SRR32217726 | Sample 3 | a mixture: K219KE, and L100LI below it |
| SRR32217715 | Sample 4 | multi-class positive: NRTI, INSTI and capsid |
| SRR32217716 | Sample 30 | fully susceptible — a clean negative |
| SRR32217714 | — | the study's no-template control |

A mixture, a strong positive, a true negative and a blank. Between them
they exercise every judgement the interpreter makes.

## What agrees

The pipeline's `mixture` parameter answers the same question as the paper's
`Amb2`/`Amb5`/`Amb15` reports, so one set of alignments validates all
three thresholds.

| Threshold | Drug calls compared | Agree |
|---|---|---|
| 15% | 81 | **81** |
| 5% | 81 | **81** |
| 2% | 81 | 72 |

At 15% and 5% the agreement is exact — every score, every level, every
mutation string, for all twenty-seven drugs on all three samples.

That includes the cases worth naming:

- **Sample 3** at 15%: `K219KE`, potential low-level resistance to
  zidovudine and stavudine. A mixture, scored as both amino acids.
- **Sample 3** at 5%: adds `L100LI` — high-level resistance to four
  NNRTIs. Same reads, same alignment; a 5% minority virus changes the
  regimen.
- **Sample 4**: `M184V`, `T97A + E138T + G140S + Q148H` giving high-level
  resistance to all five INSTIs, and `M66I + Q67H + N74D + A105T` in
  capsid giving high-level lenacapavir resistance at a score of 160.
- **Sample 30**: susceptible on all twenty-seven, with no false positives.
- **The no-template control**: all twenty-seven drugs `Not assessed`, with
  no score. Sixty of its thirty-two thousand reads map to HXB2.

## What disagrees, and why

Three of the 243 drug calls differ, all of them at the 2% threshold, and
all three are the same missed mutation counted once per drug. They are
listed with their cause in `KNOWN_AT_TWO_PERCENT` in
`tests/test_validation.py`, and the test fails if one of them changes
shape or a new one appears — which is what keeps "discrepancies explained"
from decaying into "discrepancies tolerated".

It was nine until codons were read from the reads rather than from the
combinations of their bases. Six were a phasing artefact, and
[what fixed them](#what-this-exercise-changed-about-the-pipeline) is below.

### Three are one missed mutation, and it is a real one

Stanford reports `V75VA` for Sample 30 at 2%, reading potential low-level
resistance to zidovudine, intermediate to stavudine and low-level to
didanosine. Ligase reports susceptible to all three.

The caller credits the V75A base in **4 of the 239 reads it counted at
HXB2 2773 — 1.7%**, below the threshold that would report it. HIVGenoPipe
realigns against a sample-specific reference, which is that paper's whole
method, and places reads that an alignment to HXB2 does not.

This is a sensitivity limit, not a disagreement about what the reads mean,
and it is the honest cost of the demo's approach. It is also the only
disagreement left that would change what anyone did.

Phasing does not reach it, and must not. The raw read bases at 2773 carry
the alanine base in 3.2% of themselves, where `bcftools mpileup` credits
1.7% once base quality is taken into account. Counting raw bases would
"find" V75A at a 2% threshold and would agree with the published report by
accident, on evidence the caller had already judged too poor to report.
Which alleles are present stays the caller's judgement; phasing only
decides which combinations of called alleles are real
([D31](/project/decisions/#d31--a-codon-no-read-spans-keeps-its-per-base-reading)).

## What this exercise changed about the pipeline

Validating is what found the biggest problem, and it was not in the
interpreter. It also found the second, which was.

### Codons are read from the reads, not from the combinations

Sample 3's five NRTI rows used to read `K219KEGR` where Stanford reads
`K219KE`, and Sample 4's lenacapavir row `A105AT` where Stanford reads
`A105T`: six discrepancies, every score and level identical, only the
mutation string wrong.

A codon was assembled from the VCF — the alleles credited at each of the
three bases, then every combination of them. The caller credits G at HXB2
3204 in 43% of the reads it counted and at 3205 in 2.4%, so at a 2%
threshold both pass and the combinations give `AAA`, `AGA`, `GAA`, `GGA` —
K, R, E and G. Of the 4,347 reads spanning the whole codon, 54% carry
`AAA` and 45% carry `GAA`; `AGA` and `GGA` are under two tenths of a per
cent each. **Fewer than two reads in a thousand carried both
substitutions.** A VCF says what is at each position, not what travels
together.

So codons are now read from the alignment, which was already an input for
coverage: reads spanning all three bases are counted as the triplets they
carry, and the codon holds the amino acids whose share of those reads
reaches the threshold. Six of the nine discrepancies went with it, and the
agreement at 15% and 5% is unchanged, because a majority allele is carried
by the reads that support it.

### A consensus caller could not see the minority virus (M6)

The demo's `call` step used to run `bcftools call -mv --ploidy 1`. On
Sample 3, the reads at HXB2 3204 are A 238, G 192 — **a 45% K219E** — and
`bcftools call` emitted no record for it at all. The majority allele is
the reference, so there was no variant to call. The report came back
susceptible to all twenty-seven drugs.

It was *right* to, given that input: it matches Stanford's own
majority-consensus report for the same sample exactly, which is itself
useful evidence that the interpreter is sound. But Stanford's 5% report
for that sample finds `L100I` as well and reads high-level resistance to
four NNRTIs. A pipeline whose answer flips from "fully susceptible" to
"resistant to most of the NNRTI class" on a threshold it never exposed is
not doing what it says it does.

So the caller is gone, the pileup's own allele depths are the evidence,
and `min_freq` now means what its name says.

## PRJNA517147 is not used for this

[The milestones](/project/milestones/) also name PRJNA517147, the Washington DC
cohort study (Jair et al., *PLoS ONE* 2019;14(4):e0214820). Its per-sample
resistance calls are published **only as colour in a heatmap** — Fig 4,
reverse transcriptase only, with protease and integrase "not shown" and no
supporting tables.

Of the four runs fetched from it, three are blank rows in that figure and
the fourth cannot be separated from its neighbour by eye. The mutations
the paper quantifies in text are attached to `PL-` identifiers that are
never mapped to the specimen numbers the figure uses.

That project remains the demo's default dataset, and M3, M4 and M5 are
still signed off on it. It is not used as a source of expected resistance
answers, because a number transcribed from a figure is not a citable
value. PRJNA1218827 publishes machine-readable per-sample reports with the
algorithm version stamped in every row, and that is what M6 is checked
against.
