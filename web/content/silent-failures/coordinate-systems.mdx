# My intervals are off by one

<Lede>
Your feature boundaries are all one base out. Not randomly — **consistently, by
exactly one**, at every start. Small enough that most downstream numbers barely
move, which is why it survives review; large enough to change a codon, miss the
first base of an exon, or shift a variant out of the region that was supposed to
contain it.
</Lede>

## What actually happened

You mixed two coordinate conventions. Both are correct, in their own formats:

| Format | Counts from | End | `chr1 100 200` means |
|---|---|---|---|
| BED, BAM's binary encoding, BigWig | 0 | excluded | bases 101–200, 1-based |
| GFF, GTF, VCF, SAM's text records | 1 | included | bases 100–200, 1-based |

A BED interval `100 200` and a GFF interval `100 200` describe different
stretches of sequence: 100 bases starting at the 101st, against 101 bases
starting at the 100th. They differ at the start by one and at the end by one.

The convention is a property of the *format*, not of the file, so it is never
written down anywhere in the data.

## Why nothing crashed

Both numbers are integers in range. Both intervals are well-formed. Every tool
in the chain receives two numbers it can use.

Worse, the error is mostly invisible in aggregate. Shift every exon start by one
base and total coverage changes by a fraction of a percent; a differential
expression result will move slightly and still look like a differential
expression result. The failure announces itself only at the boundary — in the
one variant that falls just outside a region, or the one reading frame that
comes out shifted.

An off-by-one that is correct 99.9% of the time is harder to find than one that
is always wrong.

## What Ligase says

<Diagnostic note="ligase check — nothing ran">{diagnostics.coordinateSystems}</Diagnostic>

Note what the sources say: *BED coordinate convention* and *GFF coordinate
convention*. The engine did not find this in the file contents, because it is
not in the file contents. It is a property of the format, inferred from it, and
therefore not something you can declare incorrectly.

The question at the end is the right one to ask. Unlike a reference mismatch,
mixing coordinate systems is often *intentional* — you do mean to intersect BED
regions with a GFF annotation. What you need is a conversion, declared as a
step, rather than the two being silently treated as comparable.

## How it is caught

`coordinates` is inferred from the format:

```python
>>> lg.intervals("peaks.bed", organism="human").dimension("coordinates").value
'0-based half-open'
>>> lg.intervals("genes.gff", organism="human").dimension("coordinates").value
'1-based closed'
```

A conversion is an ordinary typed step. Because it declares what it changes, the
graph type-checks afterwards and the conversion is recorded in provenance, which
a hand-written `awk` one-liner in a shell script would not be:

```python
@lg.step(tool="bedtools")
def to_closed(regions: lg.Intervals) -> lg.Intervals:
    """Re-express 0-based half-open intervals as 1-based closed."""
    return lg.sh("awk 'BEGIN{OFS=\"\\t\"}{print $1, $2+1, $3}' {regions} > {out}")
```

<Note kind="caution" title="A re-encoding is not a conversion">
`samtools view -b` turns a SAM into a BAM, and the BAM binary format stores
0-based offsets. That does **not** change the `coordinates` dimension: alignment
records are 1-based closed in the SAM specification, and Ligase presents them
that way from either container. The 0-based offsets inside the binary encoding
are a container detail, recorded in provenance, never as a dimension.

Reporting a lossless re-encoding as a coordinate mismatch would be a false
alarm, and false alarms train people to ignore `check()`. The reasoning is in
[the dimension catalogue](/design/dimensions/).
</Note>
