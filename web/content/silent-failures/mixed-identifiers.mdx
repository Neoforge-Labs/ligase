# My overlap with the published gene set is empty

<Lede>
You have 400 differentially expressed genes. The paper you are comparing
against has 380. You intersect them and get nine. Both files are valid, the
merge ran without complaint, and the number is low enough to be interesting —
so you start writing about poor concordance between platforms, when what you
have is a list of **symbols** intersected with a list of **Ensembl IDs**.
</Lede>

## What actually happened

A gene has many names. `TP53` is an HGNC symbol; `ENSG00000141510` is the
Ensembl gene it corresponds to; `7157` is its Entrez ID; `P04637` is the
UniProt accession of the protein. `NM_000546` is a RefSeq transcript. Each is
a correct name for the same piece of biology, and none of them matches any of
the others as a string.

Nothing in a gene list says which of those spaces it is in. A TSV with a
column called `id`, or `gene`, or `Gene`, is a column of text. The space is in
the head of whoever exported it, and in the name of the tool that did —
`featureCounts` gives you Ensembl IDs, a GEO supplementary table usually
gives you symbols, a proteomics search gives you accessions.

So the join is a string comparison between two different naming conventions.
It does not fail. A join of two columns with nothing in common returns the
rows they have in common, which is none of them, and an empty result is a
perfectly ordinary thing for a join to produce.

## Why the answer is plausible rather than absurd

If the overlap were exactly zero, you would notice. It usually is not.

Three things put a handful of rows in it. Some identifiers are valid in both
spaces: a symbol like `C1QA` and an Entrez ID are never confused, but short
numeric symbols and numeric IDs can collide. Some lists are already mixed,
because they were assembled from two sources, so a fraction of them match.
And `2-Sep` is in neither space and matches nothing anywhere.

The result is a small, non-zero overlap. That is the worst possible output: it
looks like a measurement. A zero would have sent you looking for a bug.

## The spreadsheet makes it worse

Open a list of gene symbols in Excel and it converts some of them to dates.
`SEPT7` becomes `2-Sep`. `MARCH1` becomes `1-Mar`. `DEC1`, `OCT4`, `MAR1` go
the same way. Save, and the symbols are gone — not flagged, not quoted,
replaced.

This is not a rare accident. It has been measured across the supplementary
files of published genomics papers repeatedly, at rates high enough that HGNC
eventually renamed the affected gene families to stop it happening. The
corrupted rows are then absent from every join for the rest of the analysis,
and because they are absent rather than wrong, nothing downstream has anything
to object to.

## Why nothing crashed

A merge in pandas, in R, in SQL, or in a shell `join` is a comparison of
strings. None of those tools has a concept of an identifier *space*, so none
of them can tell you that the two columns are named in different ones. They
are not being careless; they have no basis on which to object.

The same is true of the step after the join. A hypergeometric enrichment test
handed nine overlapping genes computes a p-value for nine overlapping genes.
It cannot know that the number should have been two hundred.

## What Ligase says

The identifier space is a dimension. A gene list declares it, or the loader
reads it off the identifiers themselves where they agree unambiguously — and
two lists in different spaces are refused before anything runs.

<Diagnostic note="ligase check — nothing ran">{diagnostics.mixedIdentifiers}</Diagnostic>

Three things are worth noticing.

Neither side was declared. Both say `from the identifiers' own pattern`,
naming the file: every identifier in the first matches the shape of an HGNC
symbol and nothing more specific, every identifier in the second matches
`ENSG` followed by eleven digits, so the loader filled the dimension in and
said where it got it. You can open the file it names and check.

The two spaces are printed in full, under the names the step binds them to. It
says `HGNC symbol` and `Ensembl gene`, not "identifier mismatch".

And the closing question sends you to a **step**, not to a setting:
*add a `map_identifiers` step; identifiers are never renamed implicitly*.
There is no flag that makes this join work, because going from one space to
another is a lookup that can fail, and the engine will not do a lookup behind
your back and keep the failures to itself. That is
[D38](/project/decisions/#d38--identifier-namespaces-never-coerce).

## How it is caught

Load each list as an `EntitySet`. The Ensembl side settles itself — `ENSG`
is a marker no other space has — and the symbol side says so, because bare
symbols are the shape of several spaces at once and the engine will not pick
between them
([D56](/project/decisions/#d56--a-shape-with-no-marker-is-not-a-namespace)):

```python
import ligase as lg

mine = lg.entity_set(
    "results/degs.tsv",
    organism="Homo sapiens",
    identifier_namespace="HGNC symbol",
)
published = lg.entity_set("truth/published_set.tsv", organism="Homo sapiens")

# Nothing has run. This reads the two files' identifier columns.
lg.check_step("score", {"truth": published, "found": mine})
```

That refuses with the message above.

Leave the symbol side undeclared and the refusal is the other one — the
dimension is unknown, and two lists that each say nothing are not allowed
to agree by silence:

<Diagnostic note="ligase check — nothing ran">{diagnostics.undeclaredSymbols}</Diagnostic> To get the join you wanted, convert —
which is one step, and which tells you what it could not convert:

```python
hgnc_to_ensembl = lg.entity_set(
    "refs/hgnc_to_ensembl.tsv",
    organism="Homo sapiens",
    identifier_namespace="HGNC symbol",
    release="HGNC 2026-09",
)

in_ensembl = lg.map_identifiers(
    mine,
    hgnc_to_ensembl,
    column="ensembl_gene_id",
    to="Ensembl gene",
)
```

`in_ensembl` declares `Ensembl gene`, so it joins `published` and the check
passes. Every symbol that did not cross — one the table has no row for, one it
maps to several genes — is named in the run report rather than quietly left
out. See [crossing identifier spaces](/guide/mapping-identifiers/) for the
whole worked example.

<Note kind="warning" title="A declaration is recorded, not verified">
Where you declare `identifier_namespace` yourself, the engine takes your word
for it and does not look at the identifiers. It has to: it recognises seven
spaces and there are more than seven, so an in-house list's own spelling is a
space it has never heard of and must still accept. It therefore cannot tell
that space from a wrong declaration.

What it can do is refuse to join two lists that disagree, and read the space
off the file when you leave it unset. Leaving it unset is the stronger
position where the identifiers are recognisable.
</Note>

<Note kind="note" title="And the spreadsheet damage is reported">
A list holding `2-Sep` or `1-Mar` loads, and the loader says what it saw:
`the identifier '2-Sep' is a date, not a name`. It is a note rather than an
error, because the file is valid and the fix is yours to make — re-export with
the column typed as text. It also stops the space being inferred for that
file, because one identifier the engine cannot place is a file it will not
guess about. See [what a result rests on](/concepts/notes/).
</Note>

For why a space is a dimension at all, see
[identifier spaces](/concepts/identifiers/); for how it sits alongside the
others, [the dimension catalogue](/reference/dimensions/).
